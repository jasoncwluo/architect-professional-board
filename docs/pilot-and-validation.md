# Pilot and validation

## Launch conditions

1. Agreed performance outcomes and working role expectations.
2. Diagnostic and recognition route.
3. Practice, feedback and revision designed into modules.
4. Protected time and manager-supported application opportunities.
5. Maintained environments, calibrated assessment and accountable owners.
6. Evidence-handling and publication approval.

## Illustrative schedule, not a commitment

| Stage | Example timing | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Validate outcomes and diagnose needs | Weeks 1–2 | Individual plans and agreed pilot outcomes |
| Shared foundation | Weeks 3–5 | Discovery, value, design and governance evidence |
| Role studios | Weeks 6–9 | Specialist portfolio and practical validation |
| Joint capstone and review | Weeks 10–12 | Integrated recommendation and individual decisions |
| Workplace follow-up | Approximately 30–90 days after learning | Application evidence and barriers |

The earlier 40/40/20 foundation/studio/capstone allocation is an option, not an approved baseline. Estimate duration and workload after diagnostics, scenario design and capacity review. Budget must cover learner time, mentors, assessors, lab/support capacity and content maintenance.

## Separate the causes

| Need | Response |
|---|---|
| Knowledge | Concepts and reliable reference material |
| Skill | Practice and feedback |
| Experience | Supervised work and mentoring |
| Enablement | Tools, environments, assets and experts |
| Operating model | Mandate, access, decision rights and accountability |

Maintain separate learning and structural-remediation backlogs. Do not label missing opportunity or inaccessible systems as individual incompetence.

## Evaluation

| Dimension | Proposed evidence |
|---|---|
| Competence | Change on comparable tasks using calibrated rubric dimensions |
| Application | Reviewed work examples and mentor observations |
| Sustained practice | Continued appropriate use beyond the first assignment |
| Delivery quality | Scenario relevance, feedback usefulness and lab reliability |
| Opportunity/support | Protected time, access and application opportunities |
| Engagement contribution | Specific improved decisions or avoidable issues, with attribution caveats |

Set targets after baseline measurement. Attendance, completion and confidence are supporting indicators only. Do not claim causation from programme participation without an appropriate evaluation design.

## Validation backlog

| Decision | Proposed accountable role |
|---|---|
| Business purpose and pilot audience | Sponsor |
| Role boundaries, levels and AI specialist expectations | Role leads |
| Evidence interpretation and revised-topic coding | Analysis lead and independent reviewers |
| Outcomes, prerequisites and curriculum scope | Capability owners and learning lead |
| Rubrics, critical thresholds and fairness | Assessment lead and provisional Board |
| Labs, data, access and security | Platform/data owners |
| Workload, cost and delivery capacity | Programme owner |
| Publication permissions and restrictions | Appropriate information owners |

Role assignments are proposals; confirm named accountability before launch.

## Gate before scaling

Review whether outcomes are relevant, evidence is assessable, reviewers are consistent, learning transfers and structural barriers can be addressed. Decide to revise, expand, repeat or stop. Do not formalise certification thresholds solely because a pilot cohort completed the programme.
