# Capability and enablement framework

## Interpretation

The five domains organise the proposed framework. The cumulative Top 10 / 15 / 20 areas organise enablement scope. Neither replaces approved role definitions. Numbering is a usable design sequence, not a measured importance ranking. New areas combine earlier skill themes; no earlier theme percentages should be assigned to them.

## Five domains

| Domain | Core question | Example evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Business direction and value | Are we solving the right problem? | Problem statement, capability view and value hypothesis |
| Architecture design and judgement | Why this approach rather than alternatives? | End-to-end design, options and decision rationale |
| Trust, governance and assurance | Under what conditions is the decision acceptable? | Controls, risk, readiness and accountability |
| Influence, adoption and outcomes | How will people agree, implement and use it? | Stakeholder decisions, adoption and benefit ownership |
| Professional practice and continuous learning | How do we work responsibly and improve? | Verified AI assistance, reflection, peer review and knowledge contribution |

## Top 10 — common applied foundation

| No. | Enablement area | Observable outcome | Primary domain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Business problem discovery and value framing | Validate the problem, constraints and measurable outcome before prescribing a solution | Business direction and value |
| 2 | Trusted advisory practice and stakeholder influence | Surface competing objectives, challenge honestly and facilitate an informed decision | Influence, adoption and outcomes |
| 3 | Executive communication and architecture storytelling | Explain the same decision appropriately to business and technical audiences | Influence, adoption and outcomes |
| 4 | Strategy-to-execution architecture | Connect objectives, capabilities and transition states in an executable roadmap | Business direction and value |
| 5 | End-to-end architecture and integration judgement | Make cross-domain and multi-vendor dependencies explicit | Architecture design and judgement |
| 6 | Portfolio fluency and technology selection | Compare viable options using validated capability information and dated assumptions | Architecture design and judgement |
| 7 | Data, semantics and AI-readiness assessment | Assess ownership, meaning, quality, access and contextual requirements | Architecture design and judgement |
| 8 | AI and automation use-case qualification | Justify AI, conventional automation, a combination or neither | Architecture design and judgement |
| 9 | Architecture governance, security and assurance | Identify decision rights, controls, residual risks and specialist dependencies | Trust, governance and assurance |
| 10 | Adoption-by-design and value measurement | Diagnose barriers and connect interventions, ownership and usage to outcomes | Influence, adoption and outcomes |

## Top 15 — add applied depth

Retain the Top 10 and add:

| No. | Enablement area | Observable outcome | Primary domain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | Industry, business-process and operating-model architecture | Explain business-context and operating-model implications of a proposed change | Business direction and value |
| 12 | Modernisation, clean core and technical-debt decisions | Defend what to retain, standardise, replace, extend or retire | Architecture design and judgement |
| 13 | From proof of concept to production | Identify additional evaluation, controls, operations and lifecycle requirements | Trust, governance and assurance |
| 14 | Architecture economics and commercial-model literacy | Validate cost and entitlement assumptions with accountable specialists | Business direction and value |
| 15 | Cross-role orchestration and lifecycle handovers | Preserve rationale, responsibilities and readiness across engagement phases | Professional practice and continuous learning |

## Top 20 — professional leverage and leadership

Retain the Top 15 and add:

| No. | Enablement area | Observable outcome | Primary domain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | AI-assisted architecture practice | Use AI with appropriate information handling, verification and accountable judgement | Professional practice and continuous learning |
| 17 | Architecture modelling and decision knowledge | Produce usable models and preserve assumptions, confidence and rationale | Architecture design and judgement |
| 18 | Cross-layer diagnosis and hypothesis-led problem solving | Test competing technical and behavioural explanations using evidence | Architecture design and judgement |
| 19 | Architecture practice and decision-system design | Improve architecture engagement, decision rights and delivery connections | Trust, governance and assurance |
| 20 | Reusable patterns, mentoring and knowledge stewardship | Convert experience into reviewed assets and development support | Professional practice and continuous learning |

Primary domains are navigation aids; areas contribute to multiple domains. Lower-tier placement does not mean optional ethical or professional behaviour. Areas 19–20 generally require greater emphasis at Lead level, subject to validation.

## Requirements across all areas

Practical application, feedback, continuous learning, evidence handling and appropriate collaboration apply throughout. They are not deferred until advanced modules. Deep specialist knowledge is not required equally of every role; recognising when to engage expertise is itself assessed.
