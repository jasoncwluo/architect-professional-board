# Learning and assessment blueprint

## Design logic

Evidence and business need → architecture situation → performance outcome → practice → feedback → assessed evidence → workplace application.

Every priority outcome should state: in this situation, the architect can perform this task to this standard, demonstrated by this evidence. Specify standards during validation rather than implying approved thresholds.

## Learning progression

1. Practical diagnostic and individual plan.
2. Shared architect foundation.
3. Role-specific decision studios.
4. Joint capstone with individual evidence.
5. Feedback, development actions and workplace application.
6. Follow-up and improvement.

Teach problem/outcome framing before pattern selection; establish context and readiness before advanced solution assurance. Permit demonstrated readiness instead of compulsory prerequisite attendance.

## Practice design

- Guided application: worked examples and supported use of a method.
- Independent judgement: ambiguous cases without a supplied answer.
- Adaptive judgement: revise recommendations when constraints change.

Use attempt → feedback → revision → new scenario → reflection. Distribute practice across the programme rather than relying only on one event. Do not treat a guided product demo as evidence of independent judgement.

## Module specification

Each module requires audience/prerequisites, observable outcome, role-specific depth, practice scenario, feedback method, assessment evidence/rubric, workplace application, performance support, owner and maintenance trigger.

### Example: portfolio fluency and technology selection

- Outcome: compare options with validated information and explicit trade-offs.
- Scenario: multiple plausible approaches under a constrained budget and existing investments.
- Practice: identify unknowns, validate capability claims and defend an option.
- Feedback: peer challenge and expert review, followed by revision.
- Evidence: options assessment and individual oral defence.
- Workplace application: approved engagement decision or equivalent simulation.
- Support: source-validation guide and decision template.
- Maintenance: refresh implementation examples when capabilities or prerequisites change.

## Joint capstone

Use a fictional organisation seeking to reduce order exceptions with AI despite inconsistent data, multi-platform applications, conflicting stakeholders and low trust in earlier automation. Do not use an identifiable customer account.

- Enterprise Architect: outcomes, capabilities, investments, roadmap and governance.
- Data Architect: meaning, ownership, quality, access and context.
- AI Architect: suitability, pattern, evaluation, controls and operations.
- Joint evidence: integrated design, options, risks, economics, adoption and go/no-go recommendation.

Introduce a changed constraint such as unavailable data, budget reduction or failed evaluation. Assess the resulting reasoning rather than presentation polish. Separate individual contribution from the team result.

## Assessment

Assess problem framing, evidence quality, uncertainty, trade-offs, coherence, risk, adoption and specialist depth. Use exemplars with multiple acceptable solutions, calibrated reviewers and individual defence.

Questions may include: What did you reject? Which assumption matters most? What evidence would change the recommendation?

Keep coached development distinct from formal assessment. Define reassessment, reasonable adjustments, appeals, conflicts of interest and permitted AI use. Do not allow strengths in communication to compensate for unsafe judgement in critical areas; define those areas and thresholds during calibration.

## AI-use policy for pilot design

Use approved tools and permitted data. Declare material AI assistance; verify consequential claims; retain responsibility for decisions. Assess reasoning and ability to challenge outputs rather than treating polished AI-generated artefacts as competence. Final policy requires organisational approval.

## Workplace application

Before enrolment, learner, manager and mentor agree a relevant task, protected time, expected evidence, reviewer and access constraints. Provide equivalent simulation or placement if an appropriate engagement is unavailable.

Illustrative follow-up: apply within about 30 days, mentor review around 60 days and sustained-use review around 90 days. These intervals are design options, not prescribed standards.

## Architect workbench

Provide maintained discovery questions, options template, data/context-readiness checklist, production review, decision record, adoption diagnostic and expert-navigation guide. Teach finding and validating changing facts rather than memorising them.

## Delivery ownership

- Capability owner: intended performance.
- Content owner: accuracy and maintenance.
- Lab owner: environment readiness and support.
- Assessment owner: rubric and reviewer guidance.
- Programme owner: capacity, workload and evaluation.
- Workplace sponsor: opportunity to apply.

Separate enduring principles from fast-changing implementation material. Product labs need prerequisites, limitations, validation date and update/retirement triggers.
